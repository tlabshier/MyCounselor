# Self-Talk, Inner Voice, and Neurotic Loops

Thomas Lee Abshier, ND · Isak Gutierrez | 13 October 2026

*From a morning conversation between Thomas and Isak. The exchange moved from music and growing up into an unplanned but substantive discussion about self-talk patterns, emotional suppression, and the sound of one's own consciousness.*

---

## Thomas: Growing Up Without an Emotional Register

Thomas grew up in Taft, California — desert, oil field, isolated. Both parents were only children and not worldly. No negative emotions were permitted in the household: if anyone yelled on television, the TV was turned off.

> "I literally did not know what anger was until I was about 28."

A homeopathic remedy (Sulphur), prescribed by his mentor Norman Easley, stimulated anger for the first time. Before that, Thomas describes having "essentially no emotional register — a completely flat baseline."

At age 10, the family moved to a Mobil Oil company camp in Jackson County, Texas. Thomas was beaten up for talking funny (California accent). He adapted by becoming the comedian — self-deprecating humor, the people-pleaser, "the guy no party should be without." But:

> "It was just painful. The tears of a clown."

## Isak: The Self-Deprecation Tic

Isak described a pattern he's been working to break:

- Admits ignorance as a joke → the joke becomes "I'm just an idiot"
- Developed a tic: an embarrassing memory surfaces → cringe → says "idiot" or worse out loud
- Aggressive self-talk that "you don't think you believe but half-make yourself believe through repetition"

His breakthrough came from paying attention to the *sound* of his consciousness — not the content of the thought, but what mood it carries, and what response his body should actually have to an embarrassing memory from twenty years ago.

> "It's like getting rid of neuroticism in a somewhat neurotic way."

> "As human beings we're always teetering on the edge, a couple of decisions away from the loony bin, jail, or being dead."

## Thomas: Life as States of Experience

Thomas offered a reframe:

> "This is how life is. It's not meaningless. Life populates itself with different worlds, and each is a place you can go, a thing to experience."

> "The experience doesn't define who you are, because there actually is no 'who you are.' There is only an observer and the different states you put yourself in."

## Counseling Threads

- **Emotional suppression in childhood** — Thomas's upbringing eliminated the emotional register entirely; the remedy came through homeopathic medicine, not talk therapy
- **Self-deprecating humor as survival strategy** — both Thomas and Isak used it; it works socially but installs a negative self-talk loop
- **Observing the sound of consciousness** — Isak's approach: instead of fighting the thought, listen to its tone and ask whether the body's response matches reality
- **Identity vs. experience** — Thomas's framing that the observer is not the states it passes through; identity is relational, not fixed
